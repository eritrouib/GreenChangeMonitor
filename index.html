How it works

  1. For each year it finds clear Sentinel-2 images spread across the season (the clearest from each month first) from Element 84's free Earth Search catalogue.
  2. It reads only the pixels inside the box (no downloads of whole images), masks clouds, cloud shadows and a 20 m margin around them, and keeps each pixel's greenest reading of the season (NDVI) from up to five images. Haze, missed cloud edges and grass browning in a dry spell all make places look less green, so this keeps them from showing up as loss.
  3. A pixel counts as loss only if it was clearly green (NDVI 0.5 or more), is no longer green (below 0.35) and dropped by more than the sensitivity setting, with at least two clear readings in each year. Gain is the reverse. Patches smaller than 500 m² are ignored, and the totals only count the patches shown.
  4. In England, patches are checked against conservation areas and tree preservation areas from planning.data.gov.uk.

Limits

  • Pixels are 10 m, so it sees groups of trees and lawns, not single small trees or extensions.
  • Severe drought, crops, construction sites with spoil and late spring can still look like loss, and trees felled late in the second year may only show the following year. Compare the true colour views before acting.
  • Thinning, or trees replaced by grass, stays green and isn't counted.
  • It's a pointer for where to look, not evidence on its own.
